
# Post-Workshop Office Hours

## Overview

This folder contains information, materials, questions, and follow-up discussions from the office hours organized after the **Workshop: Piloting Interoperable Hydrometeorological Data Systems for Multi-Hazard Early Warning Systems**, held in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, in September 2026.

The office hours aim to provide continued technical assistance to participating countries as they begin implementing their pilot plans and gain hands-on experience with **WIS2box and HydroServer**. These sessions also provide an opportunity for countries to share their progress, discuss challenges, ask questions, and receive technical support.

## Initial Follow-Up Meetings

The first follow-up meetings were held on **October 15 and October 22, 2026**, to accommodate participants from all four countries: **Ethiopia, Kenya, Uganda, and Rwanda**.

During these meetings, country representatives came together to share updates on their progress, discuss challenges, and identify how technical assistance could best support their work over the coming months.

The CGS team also introduced opportunities for countries to become more actively involved in the WIS2box and HydroServer communities by reporting issues, submitting feature requests, and participating in ongoing discussions. You can know more about this option in the following resources

## Office Hours Schedule

Based on feedback from the participating countries, we agreed to hold **one-hour office hours every two weeks on Thursdays**.

These sessions provide an open space for countries to discuss their progress, raise technical questions, share challenges, and receive support as they implement their pilot workflows.

**We encourage participants to submit questions or suggest topics in advance so that the technical team can prepare and make the most of each session.**

### 2. Ingesting EUMETSAT Data into HydroServer

<img src="https://flagcdn.com/w40/ug.png" width="22" alt="Uganda flag">Uganda raised a question about how to ingest data received from EUMETSAT into HydroServer.

The main challenge is decoding the messages transmitted by monitoring stations into usable hydrometeorological observations. This requires additional information about the station configuration and the format used to encode the measurements before exploring the option of ingesting this data to HydroServer.

During the session, we will share updates on our investigation and discuss the information needed to decode the data.

**Resource:**
- [Presentation: Investigation of EUMETSAT Data](https://www.canva.com/design/DAHZUQ0T_t8/d5w0A2Xhgo3jA1TRNQN-GQ/edit)
